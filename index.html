What can I download?

  • A daily bond panel from 2002-07, Enhanced and 144A TRACE combined, cleaned and error-corrected following Dickerson, Robotti and Rossetti (2026). A monthly bond asset pricing panel, including returns, credit spreads, duration, and the vast majority of characteristics the literature has used to generate the corporate bond factor zoo.
  • 108 long-short factors, single sorted and within firm, published without sign correction and explorable in FactorViz.
  • Detailed data reports that measure what each stage of the cleaning procedure removed or corrected.

How the data is built

The pipeline is public and runs in four stages, at github.com/Alexander-M-Dickerson/trace-data-pipeline.

  • Stage 0 cleans the TRACE transaction data, removing cancellations, corrections, reversals, interdealer double counts, decimal-shifted prices and bounce-back errors.
  • Stage 1 turns the cleaned trades into a daily panel with clean prices, accrued interest, yields, credit spreads, duration and convexity, and many other variables.
  • Stage 2 aggregates to the monthly asset pricing panel and estimates the characteristics, betas and other signals that comprise the bond factor zoo.
  • Stage 3 reproduces every table and figure in Dickerson, Robotti and Rossetti (2026).

Credit and duration

Over the past twenty years the greater part of the total return to holding corporate bonds is the return on the duration-matched Treasury portfolio, not compensation for credit or liquidity risk (van Binsbergen, Nozawa and Schwert (2025)).

Decomposing corporate bond returns Cumulative log return, value-weighted.

Every panel we publish carries all three Treasury hedging portfolios, so returns can be decomposed with any of the three methods. The definitions are in FactorViz, under the About tab.

Priced risk in corporate bonds

This site also hosts the corrected Bai, Bali and Wen (2019) four factors, with the code and data to reproduce them, from Dickerson, Mueller and Robotti (2023).

The paper's central finding is that the bias-adjusted sample squared Sharpe ratio of the bond market portfolio is greater than that of all four BBW factors together. From an investment standpoint it is optimal to hold the market.
